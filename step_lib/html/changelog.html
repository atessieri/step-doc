Changelog

All notable changes to step_lib are documented in this file.

3.0.0 - 2026-10-02

Aligned with step_cloud_schemas 3.0.0 and with the findings of the 2026-09-28 quality review. This release changes the wire format: every station of a deployment must move to step_lib 3.0.0 and step_cloud_schemas 3.0.0 together.

3.0.0 - Breaking/wire changes

  • ITS timestamps are TAI (5 s ahead of UTC since the ITS epoch), following step_cloud_schemas 3.0.0 ItsTime; the ISO strings of the models stay UTC.

  • SituationContainer.eventType is a CauseCodeChoice (cause code plus sub cause code), encoded as the ASN.1 ccAndScc pair.

  • CPM velocityDirection is encoded as a CartesianAngle (counter-clockwise from the East axis of the CPM frame) and converted to and from the heading from North of the model; it was sent as a heading.

  • GeoNetworking TST is TAI milliseconds modulo 2^32 (it was modulo 2^32 - 1, drifting by about 170 ms from conformant stacks).

  • Long Position Vector: S is a 15-bit signed speed (negative when reversing) and H a 16-bit unsigned heading in 0..3599; neither has an “unavailable” value any more, so an unknown speed or heading is sent as 0.

  • Confidences are rounded up (ceil) as the ETSI CDD requires, never to 0, and values beyond the largest one are sent as outOfRange.

  • AsyncComManagerParams.tls_context removed: TLS is configured only through tls_params (TLSParameters, now re-exported by step_lib), since an SSLContext cannot be pickled to reach the publish worker processes.

2.0.11 - 2026-09-23

2.0.11 - Fixed

  • PublishWorkerPool.check_health()/_restart_worker/_spawn_worker: fixed a TOCTOU race where two concurrent health-check passes (the periodic _health_check_thread and a manual SyncComManager.check_health() call) could both restart the same dead/blocked worker, orphaning one of the two spawned processes. The lifecycle (PoolState) and the bookkeeping (_workers, _ready_events, _stop_events, _heartbeats, _connected) are now guarded by one threading.Condition (_cond), restarts are bound to a _generation, and a worker slot mid-restart is tracked explicitly (_restarting) so a second concurrent pass skips it instead of racing to spawn a duplicate. A worker whose _spawn_worker call starts during close()’s snapshot window is a known, deliberately out-of-scope edge case left for a follow-up.

  • SyncComManager: replaced the boolean _async_thread_enable flag with an explicit 4-state lifecycle (_system_status: STOPPED/STARTING/ STARTED/STOPPING), checked and transitioned atomically under one threading.Condition (_cond) through _set_status(), with waiters in _wait_settled(). This closes a family of related races: two concurrent start() calls (or two concurrent stop() calls) could previously both proceed past their guard checks, the loser racing on the background thread or on duplicate teardown; a background thread that died before publishing its event loop could leave the manager permanently unable to start or stop again; and a concurrent stop() winning a race during a failed start()’s unwind could mask the original error behind the unwind’s own exception. The teardown mechanism is now a single internal _stop(), shared by stop() and start()’s failure-unwind path, that always leaves _system_status == STOPPED.

  • SyncComManager.stop(): the health-check thread join and the async loop’s stop-command dispatch are now both bounded by self._params.timeout, matching every other blocking wait in this method, instead of the health-check join being unbounded; teardown (worker pool close, final status transition) still completes even if either wait times out.

  • SyncComManager.subscribe(): a RuntimeError raised when a concurrent stop() closes the event loop between the readiness snapshot and the dispatch is now translated to StepLibError, matching the method’s documented contract; the dispatch is now bounded by self._params.timeout as well, instead of waiting indefinitely.

  • PublishWorkerPool._await_ready: the worker process is now joined (with terminate()/kill() escalation, matching check_health()’s existing blocked-worker path) after a readiness timeout, instead of being left an unreaped zombie process when _spawn_worker_with_retry immediately spawns a replacement.
