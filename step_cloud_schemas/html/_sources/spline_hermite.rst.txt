=======================================
Hermite Interpolation for Stream Gaps
=======================================

Overview
========

``GapFiller.fill()`` fills the gap between the last position of a stream and a new one with a cubic
Hermite segment for latitude and longitude, built with SciPy's ``CubicHermiteSpline``. The altitude
follows a straight line between the two positions (see `Altitude`_). No history is needed: the two
positions are enough. The list resampling (``GapFiller.resample()``) keeps the cubic spline
described in :doc:`spline_cubic`.

Why not a Cubic Spline for a Stream
===================================

A cubic spline derives the velocity at every point from its neighbours. In a stream the new
position has no following point yet, so its velocity is estimated from the previous ones. The
published segment ends with that estimate. When the next position arrives a new spline computes a
different velocity at the same point, and the next segment starts from it: heading and speed jump
at every received position, and in a turn the path bulges and returns. The history size does not
help, because the missing information is the following point.

When the positions report speed and heading, the velocity at both ends of a segment is known when
the new position arrives, with no added latency; when either is missing the chord is used (see
`Tangents`_).

Mathematical Foundation
=======================

A cubic polynomial has four coefficients, so the two positions :math:`p_A, p_B` and the two
velocities :math:`v_A, v_B` define exactly one curve. With :math:`\Delta t = t_B - t_A` and the
normalized time :math:`s = (t - t_A) / \Delta t`:

.. math::

   p(s) = h_{00}(s) p_A + h_{10}(s) \Delta t \, v_A + h_{01}(s) p_B + h_{11}(s) \Delta t \, v_B

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 15 30 15 40

   * - Weight
     - Formula
     - Weighs
     - Behavior
   * - :math:`h_{00}`
     - :math:`2s^3 - 3s^2 + 1`
     - :math:`p_A`
     - from 1 to 0
   * - :math:`h_{01}`
     - :math:`-2s^3 + 3s^2`
     - :math:`p_B`
     - from 0 to 1
   * - :math:`h_{10}`
     - :math:`s^3 - 2s^2 + s`
     - :math:`v_A`
     - zero at both ends, acts mainly at the start
   * - :math:`h_{11}`
     - :math:`s^3 - s^2`
     - :math:`v_B`
     - zero at both ends, acts mainly at the end

At :math:`s = 0` only :math:`p_A` remains and at :math:`s = 1` only :math:`p_B`, so the segment
always passes through both positions. Latitude and longitude have one polynomial each.

Worked Example
--------------

A vehicle runs a quarter circle of radius 6 m in 1 s at about 9.4 m/s, with x towards east and y
towards north: :math:`p_A = (0, 0)`, :math:`v_A = (9.4, 0)`, :math:`p_B = (6, -6)`,
:math:`v_B = (0, -9.4)`.

Tangents
========

The tangent is expressed in the units of the segment, decimal degrees per millisecond. Starting
from the position, ``Geocode.move_point_a_to_point_b()`` solves the forward geodesic problem on the
WGS84 ellipsoid for the distance travelled in one second along the reported heading, the same
ellipsoid used to recompute the output speed and heading. With :math:`(\varphi_1, \lambda_1)` the
point reached from :math:`(\varphi_0, \lambda_0)`:

.. math::

   \dot{\varphi} = \frac{\varphi_1 - \varphi_0}{1000}, \qquad
   \dot{\lambda} = \frac{\lambda_1 - \lambda_0}{1000}

The longitude difference is unwrapped, so a move across the antimeridian stays continuous. The ETSI
heading is the orientation of the velocity vector, so it already gives the direction of motion when
driving backwards and the drive direction is not used.

``GapFiller._tangent_deg_per_ms()`` uses the chord between the two positions instead when:

- speed or heading is missing;
- the speed is below ``GapFiller.TANGENT_SPEED_MIN_M_S``, where the heading is unreliable;
- the speed exceeds the chord speed by more than ``GapFiller.TANGENT_CHORD_SPEED_RATIO_MAX`` times.
  On a straight segment with equal tangents :math:`r` times the chord speed, the speed at the
  midpoint is :math:`(1.5 - 0.5 r)` times the chord speed, so above :math:`r = 3` the path goes
  backwards.

When neither position reports its motion, both tangents are the chord and the segment is a straight
line at constant speed.

Altitude
========

The schemas report no vertical speed, so the altitude has no tangent to build a Hermite segment on,
and the GNSS altitude noise (a few meters) makes a curve fitted on the history oscillate in the
gap. The interpolated altitudes are therefore a straight line between the altitudes of the two
positions (``numpy.interp``); when either position has no altitude, the interpolated positions have
none.

.. code-block:: python

   # GapFiller.fill: straight line between the two altitudes, none when either is missing
   altitudes_m = (
       np.interp(
           target_times_ms,
           [time_last_ms, time_new_ms],
           [last_reference.altitude.value, new_reference.altitude.value],
       )
       if (last_reference.altitude is not None) and (new_reference.altitude is not None)
       else np.full(shape=target_times_ms.shape, fill_value=np.nan, dtype=np.float64)
   )

Why Not for Resampling
======================

A list resampled as a whole (``GapFiller.resample()``) has every point known, so a single cubic
spline has no last point to estimate. Moreover its points may carry only time and position, so
their tangents could only come from the neighbouring points, which is less accurate than a cubic
spline on a smooth trajectory.

Implementation
==============

``GapFiller.fill()`` follows these steps:

1. **Timestamps**: The interpolated timestamps between the last position and the new one, from the
   period chosen by ``GapFiller.optimal_period()`` (see :doc:`find_period`); a ``ValueError`` is
   raised when the new position does not follow the last one
2. **Latitude and Longitude**: The Hermite segment between the two positions, evaluated at those
   timestamps
3. **Altitude**: The straight line between the two altitudes (see `Altitude`_)
4. **Output Generation**: ``GapFiller._build_chain_positions()`` computes distance, speed and
   heading of the whole chain last position -> interpolated positions -> new position with one
   batched geodesic call and builds the interpolated positions (``POSITION_GNSS_ESTIMATED`` for a
   GNSS new position, ``POSITION_PERCEIVED_ESTIMATED`` otherwise), followed by the new position
   (see `Real New Position`_)

.. code-block:: python

   # GapFiller._hermite_coordinates: both tangents known when the new position arrives
   hermite = CubicHermiteSpline(
       x=[time_last_ms, time_new_ms],
       y=[
           [last_reference.latitude, longitudes[0]],
           [new_reference.latitude, longitudes[1]],
       ],
       dydx=[last_tangent, new_tangent],
   )
   coordinates = hermite(target_times_ms)

No cubic spline is built: latitude and longitude follow the Hermite segment, the altitude a
straight line, so ``fill`` takes no history.

Real New Position
-----------------

The new position is returned unchanged when it reports both speed and heading: they are the end
tangent of the Hermite segment, so the published motion is the one the segment arrives with. When
either is missing, both are recomputed from the geodesic displacement from the last interpolated
position (or from the last position when no position is interpolated). The interpolated positions
always get the speed and heading of the displacement from the previous chain point.

The rule belongs to ``GapFiller._build_chain_positions()``, so it holds for every real position of
a chain, the resampled lists included:

.. code-block:: python

   # GapFiller._build_chain_positions: one chain point after the origin
   if real_position is not None and real_position.speed and real_position.heading:
       positions.append(real_position)              # unchanged
       continue
   speed, heading = Speed(...), Heading(...)        # displacement motion, validated
   if real_position is not None:
       positions.append(real_position.model_copy(update={"speed": speed, "heading": heading}))
       continue
   positions.append(PositionInfo(..., speed=speed, heading=heading))   # estimated

The real and the estimated positions share the same validated speed and heading, built once per
chain point from the batched geodesic call.

Usage Example
=============

.. code-block:: python

   import logging

   from step_cloud_schemas import GapFiller, GapFillerConfig

   gap_filler = GapFiller(
       config_par=GapFillerConfig(
           input_min_altitude_trailing_point_percent=25,  # used by resample only
           output_nominal_period_ms=100,  # 10 Hz output rate
           output_max_period_deviation_ms=25,
       ),
       logger=logging.getLogger(),
   )

   # last_record: last PositionInfoRecord of the stream, new_record: the new one
   positions = gap_filler.fill(
       actor=actor_id, last_record=last_record, new_record=new_record
   )

   # Interpolated positions followed by the new one
   for pos in positions:
       print(f"Time: {pos.time}, Lat: {pos.position.latitude}, "
             f"Lon: {pos.position.longitude}")

Deciding whether the gap is to be filled, that is whether the new position follows the last one
and the gap is not too long, is up to the caller: otherwise the history is dropped instead of
filled.

Performance
===========

With k interpolated positions, one call costs :math:`O(k)` in time and space: the timestamps, the
Hermite segment, the straight line, one batched geodesic call over the k + 1 pairs of the chain and
the k + 1 returned positions. The cost does not depend on the history of the stream.

Comparison with the Cubic Spline
================================

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 30 35 35

   * -
     - Cubic spline on the history
     - Hermite segment
   * - Velocity at the points
     - Computed from the neighbours
     - Reported with the position, chord when missing
   * - Data for one segment
     - The whole history
     - The two positions of the segment
   * - Last received position
     - Velocity estimated without the following point
     - Velocity known
   * - Velocity continuity across streamed segments
     - Lost: every segment comes from a different spline
     - Kept, unless a chord fallback applies
   * - Curvature continuity
     - Only within one spline
     - Not guaranteed
   * - Altitude
     - Cubic spline on the history
     - Straight line between the two positions

Limitations
===========

**Reported Motion**: The result depends on the quality of the reported speed and heading; an
inconsistency with the displacement between the positions leaves a small correction at the point.

**Curvature**: The curvature can change abruptly at a received position.

**Altitude**: The altitude is linear across the gap: a change of slope inside the gap is not
followed.

**Long Gaps**: The segment uses only its two ends, so across a long gap the path in the middle is
an assumption; deciding whether a gap is too long, and dropping the history instead of filling it,
is up to the caller.
